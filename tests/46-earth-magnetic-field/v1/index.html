A planet-sized magnet. Convection of molten iron in Earth's outer core, 3,000 km below us, drives a self-sustaining dynamo. From space its field looks almost exactly like a bar magnet's — a dipole — about 25–65 μT at the surface.

Lines from one formula. Every blue line is traced numerically from the dipole equation B = μ₀/4π · [3(m·r̂)r̂ − m] / r³. Strength falls as 1/r³ and each line obeys r = L·sin²θ: a line leaving latitude λ crests at L = 1/cos²λ Earth radii.

The solar wind pushes back. The Sun sheds plasma at 300–800 km/s. Its pressure squashes the dayside field to ~10 RE (the faint magnetopause shell) and drags the nightside into a long magnetotail. Raise the slider to watch the shield compress.

Aurora ovals. Particles that leak in gyrate around field lines; as the field strengthens near the poles their spiral tightens and some mirror back. Those reaching 100–300 km excite oxygen (green 557.7 nm, red 630 nm) in rings around the magnetic poles — tilted ~11° from the geographic ones.
